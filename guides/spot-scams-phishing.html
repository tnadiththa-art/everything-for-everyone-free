🛡️ Guide #2 · Pillar 01: Digital & AI

How to Spot Scams, Phishing & Fake Links

Cybercriminals steal millions every day not by breaking encryption, but by tricking human psychology. With modern lookalike URLs, spoofed sender names, and AI-generated messages, knowing how to verify links and defend your accounts is an essential everyday life skill.

⏱️ Time Needed: 5 mins 🎯 Difficulty: Beginner 🛡️ Focus: Cyber Defense & Privacy 📅 Published: Nov 2026
💡 What You’ll Learn In This Guide
  • How to verify suspicious URLs and identify sneaky domain spoofing before you click.
  • Why artificial panic and urgency ("account suspended in 24 hours") are dead giveaways of a scam.
  • The universal golden rule of One-Time Passwords (OTPs) and why legitimate banks never ask for them.
  • An immediate damage-control action plan if you suspect you already clicked a malicious link.
1

Inspect the Sender Email Domain (Not Just the Display Name)

Scammers often set their sender display name to "PayPal Security", "Google Support", or "Commercial Bank". Anyone can set their display name to whatever they want. What matters is the actual email address hiding behind that name.

The Sender Address Inspection Rule

Core Defense

Always click or tap the sender's name to expand the full email address. Look specifically at the part right after the @ symbol.

Real vs. Fake Example:
✅ Real Google alert: no-reply@accounts.google.com
❌ Phishing fake: security-alert@google-verify-service.com or support@paypal.security-update.net

If the domain after the @ is an extra-long hyphenated name, uses random numbers, or ends in a free service like @gmail.com or @outlook.com claiming to be a company, it is 100% fraudulent.

2

Hover & Inspect the Real URL Before Clicking

The blue clickable text in an email, text message, or social post can say anything. A link displayed as https://mybank.com/login can secretly point to a completely different server designed to harvest your credentials.

Desktop Inspection

Hover Check

Hover your mouse cursor over the link without clicking. Look at the preview URL displayed in the bottom-left corner of your browser window.

Mobile Inspection

Long-Press Check

Press and hold down on the link for 1 second. A preview card will pop up showing the actual destination URL before the browser opens it.

Lookalike Typosquatting: Watch out for sneaky letter swaps like paypa1.com (number 1 instead of l), g00gle.com (zeros), or odd extensions like .xyz, .top, or .cc masquerading as official institutions.
3

Spot Fake Urgency & Fear Tactics ("Account Suspended in 24h")

Scammers rely on adrenaline to override your analytical mind. When you feel panicked, you make hasty decisions that you would never make when calm.

Common Urgency Traps to Recognize

Red Flag Alert
  • "Your account has been suspended due to unusual activity. Click here within 24 hours to verify."
  • "Unauthorized transaction of $489.00 processed. If this was not you, call this phone number immediately."
  • "Your package cannot be delivered due to an incomplete address. Pay the 150 LKR / $2 redelivery fee now or your parcel will be destroyed."
  • "Legal summons issued against your name. Download the attachment to view the court notice."
The Out-of-Band Rule: If you ever receive an alarming message from your bank, courier, or email provider, never click the link in the message. Open a separate browser tab, manually type the official website address yourself, and log in to check your account status.
4

Never Share OTPs or Recovery Codes (The Bank Never Asks)

One-Time Passwords (OTPs) and SMS verification codes are the final barrier preventing thieves from draining your funds or locking you out of your social accounts.

🔒 The Golden Rule of Authentication

One-Time Passwords (OTPs) are meant for you to type into an app, NEVER to speak or send to another human being.

  • No legitimate bank or organization will ever ask for your OTP: Fraudsters call pretending to be your bank's security team saying, "To block an unauthorized transfer, read back the 6-digit code we just sent you." In reality, they are triggering that code to empty your account.
  • Never forward SMS verification codes: If an online "friend" or buyer messages you asking for a code sent to your phone because "their phone isn't working", they are hijacking your WhatsApp, Telegram, or Gmail account.
5

What to Do If You Already Clicked (Damage Control Protocol)

If you realize you just entered your password, credit card, or personal details on a fake site, do not waste time feeling guilty. Immediate action can prevent loss:

1. Change Passwords Immediately

Priority 1

Open a new clean tab or use another device. Immediately reset the password of the targeted account and any other accounts that used the same password.

2. Terminate Active Sessions

Priority 2

Go to your account security settings (Google, Facebook, Outlook) and select "Sign out of all other devices / sessions" to kick out the attacker.

3. Freeze Cards / Call Your Bank

Priority 3

If you entered card details or bank passwords, open your mobile banking app immediately to freeze the card, or call the 24/7 bank hotline printed on the back of your physical card.

4. Turn on App-Based 2FA

Priority 4

Switch from SMS verification to an open-source authenticator app (such as Aegis, 2FAS, or Bitwarden) so scammers cannot intercept text messages.

🚩 Quick Red-Flag Checklist: Common Scam Patterns

Watch out for these common regional and global social engineering schemes:

  • Fake Delivery / Courier SMS (Sri Lanka Post, DHL, Aramex, FedEx): Text messages claiming a package cannot be delivered due to "incorrect address information" linking to a counterfeit payment portal to harvest credit cards.
  • WhatsApp / Telegram "Part-Time Job" Offers: Unsolicited messages offering daily earnings (Rs. 10,000–50,000) for liking YouTube videos or booking hotel reviews, which eventually trick victims into transferring money into fake investment tasks.
  • Telco Free Data & Anniversary Giveaways: Viral chain messages on WhatsApp claiming "Dialog, Mobitel, or Airtel 50th Anniversary Free 50GB Data" designed to harvest phone numbers and install malware.
  • Overpayment / Fake Payment Receipt Scams: Buyers on marketplace platforms sending forged bank transfer SMS screenshots or deposit slips demanding immediate dispatch of goods.
  • Impersonation of Friends in Distress: Hacked WhatsApp accounts messaging friends asking for urgent cash transfers via eZ Cash, mCash, or quick bank transfer due to an "emergency". Always call your friend directly on the phone to verify.
